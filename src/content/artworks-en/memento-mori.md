The fragility of earthly life demands that we look our time in the eye: a profound memento mori in which our species dwells among ruin and smoke, facing the ravages of its own ambition.

We live trapped in the illusion of distance, watching the collapse of the earth as if it were someone else's horizon, ignoring that the future already lives in our present.

In the midst of that willful blindness, pure innocence sits at the edge of the abyss, shrinking the catastrophe to a hazy nightmare we believe we will wake from without consequence.

Because painting ruin is not dwelling on disaster, but raising an archive against oblivion. In every layer of oil and shadow I try to stop time, forcing the gaze through the haze of the everyday to confront what we would rather ignore.

Art thus becomes the last witness, the uncomfortable mirror revealing that there is still time to choose which land we will hand over to those who come after us.

Yet even among the cracks, a green sprout clinging to life reminds us that existence is not an unalterable fate, but an urgent warning, a reminder that what we destroy today is the inevitable inheritance of those who will come to live among the ashes.
